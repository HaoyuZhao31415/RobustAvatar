One Avatar, Any Budget:
Robust LoD for Dynamic Gaussian Avatars

NeurIPS 2026 (Poster)

Haoyu Zhao, Wenjuan Gong, Junang Qi, Haiyu Zhang, Wenjian Zhao, Zimu YOU

Abstract

Cross-device deployment of a single reconstructed avatar often requires adaptation to varying Gaussian budgets with minimal degradation in visual quality. Motivated by the need for a robust budget-adaptive 3D representation, we propose RobustAvatar, a train-once Level-of-Detail (LoD) adaptation framework for dynamic Gaussian avatars based on sequential selection and compensation. RobustAvatar supports flexible compression-ratio control from a single trained avatar, without requiring ratio-specific fine-tuning or the maintenance of separate LoD models. The framework consists of two coordinated modules: Semantic Distribution-Regularized Selection (SDS) and Canonical Budget-Conditioned Compensation (CBC). SDS performs budget-aware Gaussian selection guided by semantic distribution regularization, preserving the coverage of perceptually salient facial regions in the full avatar under constrained budgets. CBC refines the selected primitives in a canonical, expression- and pose-neutral space by predicting ratio-conditioned attribute residuals, reducing detail loss from Gaussian removal and improving robustness to decreasing Gaussian budgets. Experiments on the NeRSemble dataset show that RobustAvatar achieves more robust budget adaptation than state-of-the-art methods, with slower quality degradation as Gaussian budgets decrease. The code will be made publicly available.

Method Overview

Overview of RobustAvatar. Given a full Gaussian avatar and a target budget ratio r, Semantic Distribution-Regularized Selection (SDS) selects the top-K primitives while preserving the distribution of important facial regions. Canonical Budget-Conditioned Compensation (CBC) then predicts ratio-conditioned attribute residuals in an expression- and pose-neutral space. The compensated subset is transformed using FLAME and rasterized, enabling flexible budget control with a single trained model.
